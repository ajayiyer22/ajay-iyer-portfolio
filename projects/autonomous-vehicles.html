The problem

Lane markings alone are not enough for rural-road driving. Unmarked gravel, glare, low light, and dust make road boundaries uncertain, while asynchronous camera, LiDAR, and odometry data complicate the planner's view of the environment.

Engineering work

Frenet-frame planning

Developed a motion planner that generated candidate trajectories using lateral offsets and velocity profiles. Selected feasible candidates using curvature constraints and a weighted cost function.

Perception and coordinate alignment

Tuned CLRerNet lane detection and SAM drivable-area segmentation to supply road-boundary estimates. Aligned camera, LiDAR, and odometry data in a shared map frame for the ROS perception and planning pipeline.

Evaluation under degraded inputs

Tested using ROS bags and simulation with low light, glare, dust, unmarked gravel, sensor dropout, and timing jitter. Adjusted perception thresholds and temporal smoothing to address degraded trajectory candidates and maintain lane continuity.

Visual-token analysis

Studied visual-token redundancy in OpenDriveVLA across 6,019 nuScenes validation samples. Approximately 10 principal components captured 80% of scene-token variance and 97% of map-token variance. This suggested opportunities for token reduction; variance retention alone does not establish a deployed inference speedup.

Results & scope

The work connected road perception to feasible trajectory selection and examined how the pipeline behaved when visibility or sensor timing degraded. Evaluation used recorded data and simulation; the vehicle photographs document the AVA research platform.
