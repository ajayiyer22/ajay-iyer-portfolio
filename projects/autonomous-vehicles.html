Problem & goal

On rural roads, lane markings can be faint or absent, making it difficult to define a safe driving corridor. I developed a perception and planning workflow to estimate road boundaries and generate local trajectories. I also built a separate OpenStreetMap driver assistance prototype and studied visual feature redundancy.

Approach & implementation

  • Road perception. Integrated CLRerNet, SAM2 and SphereFormer to detect lane markings and segment road surfaces from camera and LiDAR data. Projected LiDAR into the camera frame and extracted left and right road edges from segmented point clouds.
  • Object association and planning. Associated YOLOv9 camera detections with synchronized LiDAR and radar observations. Generated Frenet trajectories using lateral quintic and longitudinal quartic polynomials, then selected references by smoothness, duration, offset and speed error.
  • Related prototypes. Built an OpenStreetMap dashboard that matches recorded odometry to route segments and provides curve, intersection and speed advisories. Separately applied PCA to OpenDriveVLA features from 6,019 nuScenes samples to measure visual representation redundancy.

Implementation & results

Delivered ROS 2 modules for road and object perception and local trajectory references, plus the separate driver assistance prototype. In the PCA study, about ten components retained 80% of scene token variance and 97% of map token variance.
