Overview

A master’s thesis comparing longitudinal spacing and lateral tracking controllers for a leader–two-follower vehicle platoon. The simulation study examines how nonlinear control choices affect tracking accuracy, convergence and control effort.

Technical contributions

  • Dynamics and estimation. Modeled the three-vehicle system in MATLAB/Simulink using nonlinear bicycle dynamics at a reference speed of 8 m/s. Implemented a Luenberger observer to estimate yaw rate from lateral velocity.
  • Controller development. Compared PID, conventional sliding-mode control, the thesis’s power-rate reaching-law formulation (PREL), and second-order super-twisting control. Evaluated both longitudinal spacing and lateral path-following behavior.
  • Performance comparison. Compared integral absolute and squared tracking errors, their time-weighted forms, and control-effort measures. This distinguishes accurate tracking from solutions that require greater actuation effort.

Results

PREL achieved the lowest error indices in the documented simulation. Super-twisting provided lateral convergence without overshoot and the lowest longitudinal control-energy metric. Related publication: ACODS 2022.
