Master's thesis · Nonlinear control

Robust control for vehicle platooning

Longitudinal and lateral control of a three-vehicle platoon, comparing PID and sliding-mode approaches through tracking error and control effort.

The problem

A vehicle platoon must maintain spacing while following a reference path. The study examined the tradeoffs among convergence, overshoot, chattering, and control effort for nonlinear vehicle dynamics.

Engineering work

Dynamics and state estimation

Modeled three vehicles using nonlinear bicycle dynamics in MATLAB/Simulink, with a reference speed of 8 m/s. Used a Luenberger observer to estimate yaw rate from lateral velocity.

Controller comparison

Implemented PID, conventional sliding mode control, power-rate exponential reaching law (PREL), and super-twisting control for the longitudinal and lateral channels.

Quantitative evaluation

Compared integral tracking-error measures and control energy across the three vehicles. Examined both path-following behavior and maintenance of longitudinal spacing.

Results & scope

PREL produced the lowest reported error indices in the comparison. Super-twisting provided lateral convergence without overshoot and the lowest longitudinal control energy. These results highlight the tradeoff between tracking accuracy and control effort in the simulated platoon.
