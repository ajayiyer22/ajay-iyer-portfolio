Robotic welding automation

Research focus

My research connected the geometry of a planned weld to its execution on a physical workpiece. I developed methods for workpiece alignment, continuous torch motion, and feedback regulation of welding conditions.

Methods and contributions

  • Rigid registration. Aligned three torch-probed points with the CAD model using the Kabsch algorithm, followed by Iterative Closest Point (ICP) refinement. This establishes the workpiece-to-robot transformation without external metrology.
  • Manipulator planning. Generated weld paths from STL edges and surface normals. Combined Newton–Raphson inverse kinematics (IK) with Rapidly-exploring Random Tree star (RRT*) planning, wrist-limit handling, and angle unwrapping to construct continuous joint trajectories.
  • Coordinated execution. Time-parameterized motion for approximately constant torch-tip speed and synchronized weld triggering with approach and retreat. Integrated planning, visualization, and welder commands in a Python/PyQt5/ROS interface.
  • Process feedback. Integrated Optris PI 05M infrared monitoring and used bead-height and calibrated temperature feedback to regulate travel speed and arc voltage, linking the observed weld-pool state to process commands.
  1. Probe reference points
  2. Register CAD
  3. Generate torch path
  4. Solve & time motion
  5. Execute & monitor

Results

The resulting workflow connects CAD geometry, robot motion, and welding-process feedback within one system, bringing geometric planning and process regulation together.
