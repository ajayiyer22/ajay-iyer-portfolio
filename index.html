About

About me

My work has taken me from a team of small robots searching for light to industrial welding, interactive robots and autonomous navigation. Across these projects, I have worked on the same practical question: how do we turn sensor measurements into useful robot motion?

At NIT Tiruchirappalli, I worked on a three-robot system that shared light measurements to find the brightest region in an arena. Our particle swarm search project led to a paper that received a Best Paper Award at IEEE ICECA 2019. During my master’s in Systems and Control at IIT Roorkee, I studied robust vehicle platooning, with related work published at ACODS 2022, and combined image processing with Kalman filtering to improve ultrasound needle tracking.

As a Robotics Engineer at MIKO, I brought that controls background to physical prototypes. I modeled mechanisms, developed expressive head motion, tuned a self-balancing robot and connected audio direction estimates to turning behavior. Working with the hardware meant testing how the models, sensors and controllers behaved together.

At Texas A&M, my Ph.D. work has included CAD-guided welding with a UR10e and perception and trajectory planning for rural roads. These projects connected geometry, camera and LiDAR processing, motion planning and hardware interfaces in working robotics workflows.

I recently joined Dr. Srikanth Saripalli’s lab, where I am building on Jackal hardware exploration and multi-robot simulation. The research scope is efficient exploration of unknown outdoor environments through task allocation based on each robot’s mobility and sensing capabilities.
